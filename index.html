Espresso Extraction

Understanding extraction parameters is the foundation of every espresso-based drink. These figures are a general guide — always follow your store's specific standards.

Water Temperature90 – 94°C
Coffee Dose18 ± 0.2g (17.8 – 18.2g)
Espresso Yield1:1.5 – 1:2 (dose to liquid)
Ristretto Yield1:1 (more concentrated)
Extraction Time~25 – 40 sec (varies)
Pressure~9 bar (machine-set)
* Grind finer = longer extraction. Grind coarser = faster. Adjust to hit your target time.
* Specific parameters vary by store — always ask your manager first.

Your Career Path

From zero to
behind the machine

A realistic step-by-step path based on real experience working in Newcastle and Sydney.

Step 01

Train at a Coffee Studio

If you're starting from scratch, a short barista course gives you the confidence and foundation to walk into a café. Look up "coffee studio" or "barista course" in your city.

  • 3-day intensive courses can take you from zero to latte art
  • Cost: approximately AUD $400 — worth it for the confidence boost
  • You'll practise on real commercial machines

Step 02

Prepare Your Resume

Keep your Australian résumé concise — one page is ideal. Highlight any customer service background, your availability (especially weekends and early mornings), and any barista or food experience.

  • Include: name, contact, availability, relevant experience
  • Mention peak-hour availability — cafés value this highly
  • No need to include a photo or date of birth

Step 03

Apply — Three Ways

Use all three channels at the same time to maximise your chances.

Most Effective

Facebook Groups

Search "your city + barista job" or "hospitality jobs" on Facebook. Local job boards post daily and are highly responsive. Owners post directly — no middleman.

Highly Recommended

Walk-In

Walk in during quiet hours (2–4pm), bring a printed résumé, and ask: "Is the manager available? I'm looking for work." A confident walk-in is often more effective than an online application.

Also Try

Seek & Indeed

Great for browsing listings and reading job descriptions. Good backup channel — apply broadly and check daily for new postings.

Step 04

Start as All Rounder → Grow to Barista

You don't need to go straight for a barista role. Starting as an all rounder gets your foot in the door, lets you learn the café environment, and often leads naturally to barista work.

  • All Rounder: table service, POS, cleaning, food prep — no coffee experience required
  • Barista: coffee-focused, requires demonstrated skills
  • Use your all rounder shifts to observe the barista, ask questions, and practise when it's quiet
~$24 / hr All Rounder
~$30 / hr Barista

Based on experience in Newcastle & Sydney. Rates may vary by employer and experience.

Interview

What to Expect in the Interview

Australian café interviews are usually relaxed and practical. Most managers won't quiz you on theory — they want to see you in action.

  • Brief chat about your experience and availability
  • You'll almost always be asked to make a coffee on the spot
  • If it goes well, you may receive a verbal offer on the day
  • Be honest about your level — it's better to say "I'm still learning latte art" than to oversell yourself

Essential English terms you'll hear and use every day working in an Australian café.

Espresso "stained" with a small amount of steamed milk or foam.

加满的长玛奇朵
Double espresso topped with steamed milk — common Aussie order.
Chai Latte
印度香料拿铁
Spiced tea concentrate with steamed milk. No espresso.
Matcha Latte
抹茶拿铁
Japanese green tea powder with steamed milk.
Babycino
儿童咖啡
Small cup of frothed milk for kids. No coffee. Usually free or $1.
Iced Latte
冰拿铁
Espresso over ice and cold milk. Common in Australian summers.
Single Origin
单一产地咖啡豆
Beans from one specific farm or region. Often used in black coffee.
House Blend
店家混合豆
A mix of beans selected by the café — usually used for milk coffees.
Portafilter
手柄 / 粉碗把手
The handle that holds the coffee grounds and locks into the machine group head.
Group Head
出水口
The part of the espresso machine where the portafilter attaches and water flows through.
Tamper
压粉器
A tool used to press and level coffee grounds evenly in the portafilter.
Steam Wand
蒸汽棒
The metal wand on the espresso machine used to heat and froth milk.
Grinder
磨豆机
Grinds coffee beans to the correct particle size. Grind affects extraction speed.
Knock Box
废渣盒
A container for knocking out spent coffee puck from the portafilter.
Milk Jug / Pitcher
奶缸
Stainless steel jug used for steaming milk. Different sizes for different drinks.
Scales
称重秤
Used to weigh the dose (input) and yield (output) for consistent espresso.
Blind Filter
清洁滤碗
A solid filter basket used for back-flushing and cleaning the group head.
POS System
收银系统
Point-of-sale terminal for taking orders and processing payments.
Crema
油脂层
The golden-brown foam that forms on top of a well-pulled espresso shot.
Pre-infusion
预浸泡
A low-pressure pre-wet of the coffee puck before full extraction begins.
Body
醇厚度
The weight or fullness of the coffee on your palate. Can be light, medium, or full.
Acidity
酸度
A bright, lively quality in coffee — think citrus or stone fruit. Not unpleasant bitterness.
Earthy
泥土感
A rich, soil-like flavour often found in Indonesian or natural-process coffees.
Fruity
果味
Bright fruit notes like berry, citrus, or tropical — common in Ethiopian beans.
Nutty
坚果风味
Warm flavours of almonds, hazelnuts, or peanuts — common in medium roasts.
Half Strength
半份浓度
Half the usual amount of coffee — same volume of milk. Gentler caffeine hit.
Double Shot
双份浓缩
Two espresso shots instead of one. Stronger coffee flavour and more caffeine.
Decaf
无咖啡因
Coffee with caffeine removed. Many cafés offer a decaf option — ask if it's available.
Extra Hot
超高温
Milk steamed above the usual 65°C. Customer may request this — be careful not to scald.
Ristretto
浓缩短萃
A short shot — 1:1 ratio. More concentrated and sweeter than a standard espresso.
Smooth
顺滑
Low acidity, balanced body — easy to drink. A common customer preference.
Strong
浓
More coffee flavour — usually means an extra shot. Always confirm with the customer.
